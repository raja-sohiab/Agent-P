# Email 1 — Commercial Reasoning Patterns

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the recurring commercial reasoning patterns Agent-P can use when generating Email 1.

Patterns help the agent determine:

- What situation to focus on
- Who the relevant audience is
- Why that audience may need the company's service
- How PersistIQ can be connected to that opportunity

Patterns are reasoning tools.

They are not writing templates.

The agent must select the pattern that best fits the actual business.

---

# 2. Core Principle

Do not start by asking:

"What example looks most similar to this lead?"

Start by asking:

"What commercial situation naturally connects this business to the customers it wants to reach?"

Then select the most appropriate reasoning pattern.

---

# 3. Pattern 1 — Problem → Service

## Reasoning

Customer has a problem or need
→ Company provides the service that addresses it
→ Relevant customers can be reached through outbound prospecting

## Typical Businesses

- Home services
- Professional services
- Repair businesses
- Healthcare services
- Technology services
- Maintenance providers

## Typical Situations

- Repair
- Damage
- Ongoing problem
- Service requirement
- Pain point
- Need for professional help

## Example Reasoning

Roofing company:

Property owner
→ aging or damaged roof
→ needs roofing service
→ roofing company
→ relevant prospects
→ PersistIQ

The final email should focus on the commercial situation, not simply describe the company.

# 11. Pattern 9 — Audience Need → Conversation

## Reasoning

A recognizable audience commonly needs the company's service
→ company benefits from reaching that audience
→ PersistIQ can help create relevant conversations

This is useful when a specific commercial trigger cannot be established.

## Example

Dental practice:

People considering dental treatment
→ need appropriate dental care
→ dental practice provides that service
→ PersistIQ could help create conversations with relevant prospects

This pattern should remain specific enough to avoid generic messaging.

# 12. Pattern 10 — Industry-Specific Commercial Trigger

Some businesses operate in markets where a particular commercial trigger is especially relevant.

Examples may include:

- Property transactions
- Construction projects
- Technology adoption
- Equipment replacement
- Financial planning
- Healthcare decisions
- Business expansion
- Regulatory requirements

Use an industry-specific trigger when it is strongly connected to the company's service.

Do not invent a trigger merely because it sounds commercially interesting.

---

# 13. Pattern Selection

The agent should consider:

1. What does the business provide?
2. Who needs it?
3. What situation causes that customer to need it?
4. Which pattern best represents that situation?
5. Can the situation be supported by available information?
6. Can PersistIQ naturally help the business reach that audience?

Select the strongest logical pattern.

Do not select a pattern simply because it appears frequently in the examples.

---

# 14. Multiple Possible Patterns

Some businesses may fit multiple patterns.

For example, a software consultancy may fit:

- Evaluation → Specialist
- Problem → Service
- Growth → Business Need
- Decision → Specialist

Choose the pattern that creates the clearest and most natural Email 1.

Prefer the pattern that:

- Requires fewer assumptions
- Is most closely connected to the company's actual service
- Identifies a clear audience
- Creates a natural PersistIQ connection

---

# 15. Avoid Forced Angles

Do not force a business into a pattern when the relationship is weak.

Weak reasoning:

Company
→ random industry trend
→ unrelated customer situation
→ PersistIQ

Strong reasoning:

Company service
→ customer need
→ relevant audience
→ opportunity to reach that audience
→ PersistIQ

---

# 16. Website Information and Pattern Selection

When website information is available, use it to determine the strongest pattern.

Example:

Website says:
"AI solutions for food manufacturers."

Possible reasoning:

Food manufacturers
→ operational challenges
→ evaluating AI/automation
→ AI/software expertise
→ relevant businesses
→ PersistIQ

The website information makes the Evaluation → Specialist pattern particularly appropriate.

---

# 17. Unknown Business Types

If the business does not fit any existing pattern:

Do not reject it.

Instead:

1. Understand the business.
2. Identify its service.
3. Identify its audience.
4. Identify the most plausible customer need.
5. Create a logical commercial relationship.
6. Use the closest pattern.
7. Adapt the reasoning rather than forcing the wording.

Patterns are expandable.

---

# 18. Pattern and Reference Examples

The 500 Email 1 examples may demonstrate patterns that are not explicitly listed here.

When an example reveals a useful recurring reasoning pattern:

- Identify the underlying commercial logic
- Document the pattern
- Do not copy the example's wording
- Apply the reasoning to future businesses

The examples remain the primary reference set for discovering useful patterns.

---

# 19. Pattern Does Not Determine Wording

A pattern determines the reasoning.

It does not determine:

- Exact sentence structure
- Exact verbs
- Exact CTA
- Exact wording
- Exact sentence length

The agent should use the pattern to understand the business and then write naturally.

---

# 20. Final Pattern Test

Before generating Email 1, Agent-P should be able to answer internally:

"What customer situation makes this company's service relevant, and why would reaching those customers through outbound prospecting make sense?"

If that question cannot be answered logically, the agent should reassess its business understanding before writing.

---

# 21. Core Pattern Model

All patterns ultimately follow:

Customer type
→ Situation
→ Need
→ Service
→ Relevant customer type
→ PersistIQ Value

Use the actual customer type in the Email 1 offer when the business and service evidence supports it. Do not use “prospect” as a generic substitute, and do not force or invent a customer type.

The pattern determines how the situation is framed.

The underlying commercial logic must remain sound.
