# Email 1 — Quality Assurance

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the final QA process Agent-P must perform before approving Email 1.

QA exists to catch:

- Incorrect business understanding
- Weak relevance
- Unsupported assumptions
- Generic messaging
- Product misalignment
- Repetition
- Poor language
- Excessive length
- Aggressive CTAs
- Deliverability issues

An email should not be returned until it passes the relevant checks.

---

# 2. Production Word-Count Check

Email 1 must contain 15–25 words. This is a hard production requirement.

Check the word count during final QA. If Email 1 falls outside 15–25 words, rewrite it before release.

---

# 3. Business Understanding Check

Confirm:

- The company has been understood correctly.
- The service/product is represented accurately.
- The business category is reasonable.
- Website information has been used when available.
- No unrelated business assumption has been introduced.

If the business is misunderstood, rewrite the email.

---

# 3. Audience Check

Confirm:

- The audience logically needs or may need the company's service.
- The audience is relevant to the commercial situation.
- The audience is not chosen merely because it appeared in a reference example.

Ask:

"Who would realistically buy or use this company's service?"

The email should connect to that audience.

---

# 4. Situation Check

Confirm that the email identifies a plausible situation.

Examples:

- Planning
- Evaluation
- Repair
- Replacement
- Maintenance
- Growth
- New project
- Upgrade
- Purchase
- Business need
- Service requirement

The situation must logically connect the audience to the company's service.

---

# 5. Service Check

Confirm:

- The customer's situation creates a logical need for the company's service.
- The service is represented accurately.
- The email does not imply services the company does not provide.

Do not generalize the company so much that the service becomes meaningless.

---

# 6. PersistIQ Value Check

Confirm that PersistIQ's value is relevant to the business.

The email should make a logical connection:

Business service
→ Relevant audience
→ Opportunity to reach that audience
→ PersistIQ

The product should not feel inserted artificially.

---

# 7. CTA Check

Confirm:

- The CTA is soft.
- The CTA is easy to respond to.
- The CTA does not pressure the prospect.
- The CTA does not demand a meeting.

Preferred CTA concepts:

- Worth exploring?
- Open to seeing how?
- Worth a look?
- Open to exploring it?
- Would it make sense to explore?
- Make sense to discuss?

---

# 8. Length Check

Email 1 should normally:

- Contain two sentences
- Be concise
- Be easy to scan
- Avoid unnecessary explanation

Remove unnecessary words.

Do not sacrifice clarity simply to make the email shorter.

---

# 9. Relevance Check

Ask:

"Could this email reasonably have been written specifically for this type of business?"

If the email could be sent unchanged to hundreds of unrelated businesses, it is too generic.

Rewrite it using stronger business reasoning.

---

# 10. Naturalness Check

Read the email as if you were the recipient.

Ask:

- Does it sound like a real salesperson?
- Does the transition between sentences make sense?
- Does the product value naturally follow the business situation?
- Does anything sound forced?
- Does anything sound like AI-generated copy?

If the email feels scripted, rewrite it.

---

# 11. Fabrication Check

Verify that the email does not invent:

- Customers
- Projects
- Growth
- Revenue
- Partnerships
- Expansion
- Current initiatives
- Customer demand
- Company problems
- Current plans
- Specific company activity

unless supported by available information.

---

# 12. Inference Check

Identify statements that are inferred rather than explicitly known.

If an inference is reasonable, use appropriate language.

Preferred:

"Businesses evaluating..."

"Property owners considering..."

"People looking for..."

"Companies that may be..."

Avoid unsupported certainty:

"Your customers are..."

"You're currently..."

"Your company needs..."

unless directly supported.

---

# 13. Personalization Check

Confirm that personalization comes from business relevance.

Good:

- Correct service
- Correct audience
- Relevant situation
- Website-supported business understanding

Bad:

- Random website detail
- Generic compliment
- Unnecessary company-name repetition
- Fake observation
- Pretend familiarity

---

# 14. Reference Example Check

Compare the generated email against the 500 reference examples for reasoning quality.

Do NOT check whether the email is similar enough to a particular example.

Instead ask:

- Does it use the same quality of reasoning?
- Is the commercial situation clear?
- Is the service connection logical?
- Is the PersistIQ value integrated naturally?
- Is the tone consistent?

The examples are references, not templates.

---

# 15. Copying Check

The email must not:

- Copy a reference example
- Copy a previous lead's email
- Perform simple noun substitution
- Reproduce a recognizable sentence pattern unnecessarily

If wording is too close to another example, rewrite it.

---

# 16. Language Check

Verify that the email:

- Uses simple language
- Is conversational
- Is professional
- Is concise
- Avoids jargon
- Avoids hype
- Avoids unnecessary adjectives
- Avoids corporate language

---

# 17. AI-Style Check

Reject or revise if the email contains unnecessary phrases such as:

- I noticed...
- I came across...
- I saw that...
- I was impressed by...
- I wanted to reach out...
- As a leading...
- In today's world...

The email should begin with the relevant business/customer situation whenever possible.

---

# 18. Hype Check

Reject language such as:

- Game-changing
- Revolutionary
- Explosive growth
- Skyrocket
- Dominate
- Massive
- Incredible
- Amazing
- Cutting-edge
- Best-in-class
- 10x
- Supercharge

The value should be understated.

---

# 19. Guarantee Check

Reject any statement that guarantees:

- Leads
- Meetings
- Customers
- Revenue
- Sales
- Replies
- Appointments
- Conversions
- Pipeline
- Results

Use cautious positioning.

---

# 20. Deliverability Check

Email 1 should remain clean.

Avoid unnecessary:

- Links
- Images
- Promotional formatting
- Excessive punctuation
- Spam-like terminology
- Promotional claims

Do not add calendar links.

---

# 21. Two-Sentence Test

The preferred final format is:

Sentence 1:
Business relevance

Sentence 2:
PersistIQ value + soft CTA

If more than two sentences are used, determine whether they are genuinely necessary.

Default back to two sentences whenever possible.

---

# 22. Logical Flow Test

Verify:

Business
↓
Audience
↓
Situation
↓
Service
↓
PersistIQ value
↓
CTA

Every step does not need to be explicitly stated.

However, the underlying logic must be present.

If the transition from the business situation to the PersistIQ value feels disconnected, rewrite the email.

---

# 23. Generic Email Test

Reject emails that could apply equally to unrelated businesses.

Weak:

"Businesses looking to grow need more customers. PersistIQ could help generate more leads."

This does not demonstrate business understanding.

Better:

Use the specific customer audience, service, and commercial situation associated with the business.

---

# 24. Softness Test

The prospect should be able to ignore the email without feeling pressured.

Good:

"Worth exploring?"

Bad:

"Can we schedule a 15-minute call this week?"

---

# 25. Final Recipient Test

Read the email from the prospect's perspective.

Ask:

"If I received this email, would I understand why the sender thinks this could be relevant to my business?"

If no, revise.

---

# 26. Final Approval Checklist

Before returning Email 1:

[ ] Business understood correctly

[ ] Website information used when available

[ ] Audience is logical

[ ] Customer situation is plausible

[ ] Service connection is clear

[ ] PersistIQ value is relevant

[ ] CTA is soft

[ ] Email is concise

[ ] Normally two sentences

[ ] Human tone

[ ] No AI-style opener

[ ] No hype

[ ] No guarantees

[ ] No fabricated claims

[ ] Inferences are appropriately cautious

[ ] No fake personalization

[ ] No unnecessary links

[ ] No calendar link

[ ] No excessive punctuation

[ ] No copied reference example

[ ] No unnecessary repetition

[ ] Email makes logical sense without additional explanation

---

# 27. Final Quality Gate

An Email 1 is approved only when all critical checks pass.

The standard is not:

"Does this sound like an email?"

The standard is:

"Does this demonstrate accurate business understanding, establish a plausible commercial situation, connect naturally to PersistIQ's value, and invite a response without pressure?"

If not, revise.
