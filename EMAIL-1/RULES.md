# Email 1 — Rules

## 1. Purpose

Email 1 is the first outbound message in the sequence.

Its job is to establish:

1. Why the message is relevant to the business
2. What customer situation or need is relevant
3. How the company's service connects to that situation
4. How PersistIQ could help create relevant sales conversations
5. A natural reason to respond

Email 1 should establish relevance before presenting the PersistIQ value.

---

## 2. Core Structure

The core reasoning is:

Business
→ Audience
→ Customer situation / need
→ Relevant service
→ PersistIQ value
→ Soft CTA

The final email should normally express this reasoning in a concise two-sentence message.

The structure is a reasoning framework, not a fixed template.

---

## 3. Business Understanding Comes First

Before writing Email 1, understand the business.

Determine:

- What the company does
- What it sells or provides
- Who it serves
- What type of customer may need its service
- What commercial situation could lead that customer to seek the service

Do not write the email until the business context is sufficiently understood.

---

## 4. Use Website Information

When a website is available, use it to improve business understanding.

Prioritize information about:

- Services
- Products
- Customer types
- Industries served
- Problems addressed
- Solutions provided
- Commercial use cases

Website information should improve relevance.

Do not insert website details merely to make the email appear personalized.

---

## 5. Audience Identification

Identify the most logical customer or buyer audience for the business.

Examples:

A roofing company:
→ property owners / businesses with roofing needs

An accounting firm:
→ businesses needing accounting or financial support

A dental practice:
→ people considering dental services

A software consultancy:
→ businesses evaluating software, automation, or technology solutions

A real estate company:
→ buyers, sellers, investors, landlords, or other relevant property participants

The audience must logically connect to the company's actual service.

---

## 6. Customer Situation

Identify a plausible situation in which the audience may need the company's service.

Possible situations include:

- Planning
- Evaluation
- Replacement
- Maintenance
- Growth
- New project
- Problem resolution
- Service need
- Purchase decision
- Business change
- Upgrade
- Expansion
- Ongoing requirement

Use the situation that best fits the business.

Do not force a situation simply because it appeared in an example.

---

## 7. Service Connection

The customer situation must connect naturally to the company's service.

The email should make logical sense:

Customer situation
→ Need
→ Company's service

The service should not appear randomly.

Avoid generic descriptions that could apply equally to unrelated businesses.

---

## 8. PersistIQ Connection

After establishing business relevance, connect the situation to PersistIQ.

The value should communicate that PersistIQ could help the business:

- Create more relevant conversations
- Reach relevant prospects
- Reach people considering the service
- Reach businesses considering the service
- Surface relevant opportunities
- Connect with potential customers

The product value should feel like the natural next step in the reasoning.

---

## 9. Soft CTA

Email 1 should end with a low-pressure CTA.

The CTA should invite consideration or conversation rather than pressure the prospect.

Examples of CTA concepts:

- Worth exploring?
- Open to seeing how?
- Would it make sense to explore?
- Open to exploring it?
- Worth a look?
- Make sense to discuss?

Do not force the exact same CTA into every email.

Vary naturally.

---

## 10. Length

Email 1 should normally be:

- 2 sentences
- Short
- Concise
- Easy to read quickly

Do not add unnecessary background information.

Do not explain the entire product.

Do not turn the email into a sales pitch or product description.

---

## 11. Sentence Roles

A strong default structure is:

### Sentence 1

Establish business-specific relevance.

Include the appropriate combination of:

- Audience
- Situation
- Need
- Service

### Sentence 2

Connect the situation to PersistIQ's value and finish with a soft CTA.

Conceptually:

Sentence 1:
Relevant business/customer situation.

Sentence 2:
PersistIQ value + soft CTA.

This structure may be adapted when another arrangement produces a more natural email.

---

## 12. The 500 Reference Examples

The 500 PersistIQ Email 1 examples are the primary reference material for Email 1 style and reasoning.

They demonstrate:

- Business-type interpretation
- Audience selection
- Commercial situations
- Service relevance
- Sentence structure
- Concision
- Tone
- PersistIQ value integration
- CTA style

The examples must be treated as reference knowledge.

They are NOT templates.

---

## 13. Do Not Copy Examples

Never:

- Copy an example verbatim
- Copy an example and replace the business name
- Copy an example and replace the service
- Perform simple noun substitution
- Force a new lead into an existing example

Instead:

Understand why the example works
→ identify the underlying reasoning
→ apply that reasoning to the new business.

---

## 14. Generalization

The agent must be able to handle business types that are not represented in the 500 examples.

When encountering an unfamiliar business:

1. Understand the business.
2. Identify its primary service.
3. Identify its likely audience.
4. Identify a plausible customer situation.
5. Find the closest logical reasoning pattern.
6. Adapt the reasoning to the actual business.
7. Apply the PersistIQ value.
8. QA the result.

Do not reject an unfamiliar business simply because it is absent from the examples.

---

## 15. Evidence and Inference

The email may use reasonable category-level inference.

For example:

A roofing business commonly serves property owners who may need repairs or replacement.

This is a reasonable business-category inference.

However, do not claim:

"The company is currently looking for roofing projects."

unless that fact is actually supported.

Use cautious language for inferred situations:

- may
- might
- could
- often
- people considering
- businesses evaluating
- teams looking for

---

## 16. No Fabrication

Never invent company-specific information.

Do not claim the company:

- Has a particular customer
- Is currently growing
- Recently launched something
- Has a current project
- Is expanding
- Is hiring
- Has a particular problem
- Is actively looking for customers
- Serves a specific customer type

unless the information is supported.

---

## 17. No Fake Personalization

Personalization must come from genuine business relevance.

Good:

"Businesses evaluating custom software often need..."

Bad:

"I noticed your amazing company is transforming..."

The goal is to understand the business, not flatter the prospect.

---

## 18. Tone

Email 1 should feel:

- Human
- Professional
- Conversational
- Direct
- Calm
- Commercially aware
- Low pressure

Avoid:

- Hype
- Excessive enthusiasm
- Marketing language
- Corporate jargon
- Long explanations
- Aggressive sales language

---

## 19. Avoid AI-Style Openings

Avoid openings such as:

- I noticed...
- I came across...
- I saw that...
- I was impressed by...
- As a leading...
- In today's world...
- I wanted to reach out because...

Start with the business relevance instead.

---

## 20. Avoid Generic Value Statements

Avoid statements that could apply to almost any business.

Weak:

"PersistIQ can help you generate more leads."

Better:

"PersistIQ could help create more conversations with businesses considering that service."

The value should be connected to the business's actual customer audience.

---

## 21. No Guarantees

Never guarantee:

- Leads
- Meetings
- Customers
- Sales
- Revenue
- Replies
- Appointments
- Conversions
- Pipeline
- Results

Use "could help" or similarly cautious positioning when appropriate.

---

## 22. No Aggressive Language

Do not use:

- Guaranteed
- Dominate
- Skyrocket
- Explode
- Massive growth
- 10x
- Close more deals
- Never miss another lead
- Get flooded with leads

The messaging should remain understated.

---

## 23. Deliverability-Aware Writing

Email 1 should remain clean and simple.

Avoid unnecessary:

- Links
- Images
- Formatting
- Promotional language
- Excessive punctuation
- Spam-like wording

Do not add a calendar link.

---

## 24. Repetition Control

Avoid producing every Email 1 with exactly the same wording.

Variation should be natural.

Vary:

- Sentence openings
- Audience descriptions
- Situation framing
- Verbs
- Value phrasing
- CTA wording
- Sentence rhythm

Do not randomly change words just to create variation.

---

## 25. Final Relevance Test

Before approving Email 1, ask:

"If I removed the company name and looked only at the email, would the business-specific reasoning still make sense?"

If the answer is no, revise the email.

The email must be relevant because the underlying reasoning is relevant.

---

## 26. Final Quality Standard

A strong Email 1 should satisfy:

Business understood
+
Audience makes sense
+
Situation is plausible
+
Service connection is logical
+
PersistIQ value is relevant
+
CTA is soft
+
Email is concise
+
Language feels human
+
No unsupported claims
+
No template copying

Only return the email after it passes these checks.
