# Email 2 — Follow-Up Patterns

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the primary ways Agent-P can construct Email 2 from the actual Email 1.

These patterns are follow-up reasoning patterns.

They are not fixed templates.

The agent should choose the pattern that best preserves the original Email 1 proposition while making the follow-up shorter and more direct.

---

# 2. Core Principle

Email 2 should not ask:

"What new thing can I say?"

It should ask:

"What is the strongest part of Email 1 worth reinforcing?"

Then express that idea concisely.

---

# 3. Pattern 1 — Core Opportunity Reminder

## Reasoning

Email 1 identifies a relevant opportunity.

Email 2 briefly reminds the prospect of that opportunity.

### Example

Email 1:

"Property owners dealing with aging roofs may be comparing inspection and replacement options. We could help create more conversations with people considering that work."

Email 2:

"Could help reach more property owners considering roofing work. Worth exploring?"

The core opportunity remains intact.

---

# 4. Pattern 2 — Audience + Service Reminder

## Reasoning

Focus on the audience and the service they may be considering.

### Example

Email 1:

"Businesses outgrowing basic bookkeeping may need more structured accounting support. We could help create more conversations with owners considering that support."

Email 2:

"Could help reach more business owners considering accounting support. Worth a look?"

This pattern works when the audience-service relationship is the strongest part of Email 1.

---

# 5. Pattern 3 — Situation + Value Reminder

## Reasoning

Briefly retain the customer situation while emphasizing the PersistIQ opportunity.

### Example

Email 1:

"Companies evaluating automation often need technical support to turn those plans into working systems. We could help create conversations with businesses exploring that route."

Email 2:

"Could help reach businesses exploring automation support. Open to seeing how?"

This works when the situation is commercially important.

---

# 6. Pattern 4 — Service + Prospect Opportunity

## Reasoning

Reduce the original proposition to:

Relevant service
→ relevant prospects
→ PersistIQ value

### Example

Email 1:

"Homeowners planning renovations often look for designers who can turn their ideas into a cohesive space. We could help create more conversations with people considering that guidance."

Email 2:

"Could help connect your design services with homeowners planning renovations. Worth exploring?"

Use this when the service itself is the clearest anchor.

---

# 7. Pattern 5 — Audience + Conversation

## Reasoning

Focus primarily on reaching the right audience and creating conversations.

### Example

Email 1:

"Manufacturers reviewing production systems may need specialized equipment and support. We could help surface conversations with businesses evaluating those requirements."

Email 2:

"Could help create more conversations with manufacturers evaluating equipment needs. Worth a look?"

This pattern is useful when the audience is highly specific.

---

# 8. Pattern 6 — Opportunity + Soft CTA

## Reasoning

Compress Email 1 into one direct opportunity statement followed by a soft CTA.

### Example

"Could help reach businesses considering your software services. Worth exploring?"

This is one of the shortest acceptable forms.

Use it only when enough context remains.

---

# 9. Pattern 7 — Reinforced Value

## Reasoning

Keep the business situation extremely concise and emphasize the relevant PersistIQ value.

### Example

"Could help surface more conversations with businesses considering your services. Open to seeing how?"

Use when Email 1 already established the business context clearly.

---

# 10. Pattern 8 — Slightly Reframed Reminder

## Reasoning

Keep the same commercial proposition but describe it from a slightly different wording perspective.

Example:

Email 1:

"Property managers comparing contractors for seasonal HVAC maintenance may be looking for reliable support. We could help bring your team into more of those decisions."

Email 2:

"Could help put your HVAC services in front of more property managers reviewing maintenance options. Worth exploring?"

The commercial proposition remains the same.

---

# 11. Pattern Selection

Choose the pattern based on what is strongest in Email 1.

Ask:

1. What is the clearest commercial idea?
2. Who is the relevant audience?
3. What situation matters most?
4. What service is being discussed?
5. Which element can be retained while removing unnecessary detail?

Then choose the simplest natural structure.

---

# 12. One-Sentence Preference

If the core proposition can be expressed clearly in one sentence, prefer one sentence.

Example:

"Could help reach more business owners considering accounting support. Worth exploring?"

Do not add another sentence just because Email 1 had two sentences.

---

# 13. Two-Sentence Option

Use two sentences when the first sentence needs a short reminder before the value/CTA.

Example:

"Business owners considering more structured accounting support may be worth reaching. We could help create more of those conversations. Worth exploring?"

Keep both sentences short.

---

# 14. Pattern Adaptation

Patterns can be combined when necessary.

For example:

Audience + Situation
+
PersistIQ Value
+
CTA

But avoid combining too many elements.

The goal is compression.

---

# 15. Do Not Introduce New Information

Patterns must work with information already established in Email 1.

Do not use a pattern as an excuse to introduce:

- New customer segments
- New services
- New business problems
- New company facts
- New product claims

unless verified new information is explicitly supplied.

---

# 16. Pattern vs Template

A pattern describes reasoning.

A template dictates wording.

Agent-P should use patterns, not templates.

For example:

Pattern:

Audience + service opportunity + PersistIQ value

Not:

"Could help reach more [audience] considering [service]. Worth exploring?"

The latter may occasionally occur naturally, but it must not become a mechanical template.

---

# 17. Avoid Pattern Repetition

Do not force the same pattern across every Email 2.

Choose the pattern that best fits the actual Email 1.

However, do not sacrifice natural writing merely to create variation.

---

# 18. Preserve Original Meaning

A successful pattern transformation changes the length and wording.

It does not change the commercial proposition.

Example:

Email 1:
Businesses evaluating automation.

Email 2:
Businesses exploring automation.

Same idea.

Not:

Email 2:
Businesses looking for accounting support.

Different idea.

---

# 19. Pattern Priority

When several patterns are possible, prioritize:

1. Clear business relevance
2. Strong audience connection
3. Strong service connection
4. Clear PersistIQ value
5. Concision
6. Natural wording

---

# 20. Avoid Generic Compression

Do not compress Email 1 so aggressively that the result becomes generic.

Bad:

"Could help you reach more prospects. Worth exploring?"

Better:

"Could help reach more property owners considering roofing work. Worth exploring?"

The second preserves meaningful context.

---

# 21. Avoid Over-Compression

Do not remove the entire customer situation if it is necessary to understand the opportunity.

The agent should retain the smallest amount of context needed for relevance.

---

# 22. Avoid New-Angle Behavior

Email 2 patterns should not become Email 3 patterns.

Email 2:

Reinforce the original proposition.

Email 3:

Introduce a new relevant reason.

Keep the stages distinct.

---

# 23. Soft CTA Pattern

The CTA should remain short.

Preferred concepts:

- Worth exploring?
- Worth a look?
- Open to seeing how?
- Open to exploring it?
- Make sense to discuss?

Do not add:

- Meeting requests
- Calendar links
- Scheduling instructions
- Urgency

---

# 24. Final Pattern Test

Before approving Email 2, ask:

### Continuity

Does the pattern clearly come from Email 1?

### Relevance

Is the original business context still visible?

### Compression

Has unnecessary information been removed?

### Value

Is the PersistIQ value still clear?

### Originality

Is the wording different enough from Email 1?

### CTA

Is the CTA soft?

### Sequence

Is this still a follow-up rather than a new angle?

If yes, the pattern is appropriate.

---

# 25. Core Pattern Model

Email 2 should generally follow:

Original Proposition
→ Select Strongest Element
→ Compress
→ Rephrase
→ PersistIQ Value
→ Soft CTA

The pattern determines how the proposition is compressed.

The commercial meaning must remain consistent.
