# Email 2 — Quality Assurance

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the final QA checks Agent-P must perform before approving Email 2.

Email 2 must function as a genuine follow-up to the actual Email 1.

The QA process should catch:

- Sequence drift
- Excessive repetition
- Weak continuity
- Loss of business context
- Generic messaging
- New unsupported claims
- New unrelated angles
- Excessive length
- Aggressive CTAs
- Artificial follow-up language

---

# 2. Source Check

Confirm that the actual Email 1 was available and reviewed before generating Email 2.

If Email 1 is available, Email 2 must be based on it.

Do not approve an Email 2 that appears to have been independently generated from the lead without considering Email 1.

---

# 3. Core Proposition Check

Identify the central proposition of Email 1.

Ask:

"Does Email 2 still communicate the same underlying commercial opportunity?"

If no, revise it.

---

# 4. Business Context Check

Confirm that Email 2 remains connected to the same business and service.

The recipient should still understand what type of opportunity is being discussed.

---

# 5. Audience Check

Confirm that the relevant audience from Email 1 remains consistent.

Do not switch audiences without a legitimate reason.

Example:

Email 1:
Property owners considering roofing work.

Email 2:
Property owners considering roofing work.

Correct.

---

# 6. Situation Check

Confirm that Email 2 remains connected to the original customer situation.

It may rephrase the situation.

It should not replace it with an unrelated problem.

---

# 7. Service Check

Confirm that the company's relevant service remains logically connected to the follow-up.

Do not introduce a different service simply to create variation.

---

# 8. PersistIQ Value Check

Confirm that the PersistIQ value remains consistent with Email 1.

The wording may change.

The underlying value should not.

Examples:

- Create relevant conversations
- Reach relevant prospects
- Surface relevant opportunities
- Connect with potential customers

---

# 9. Follow-Up Check

Ask:

"Does this feel like a follow-up to Email 1?"

A good Email 2 should feel like the sender is continuing the original conversation.

It should not feel like a new cold email.

---

# 10. Compression Check

Email 2 should normally be shorter or simpler than Email 1.

Ask:

"Has unnecessary information from Email 1 been removed?"

Remove:

- Secondary details
- Repeated explanations
- Extra services
- Long context
- Product explanation

---

# 11. Brevity Check

Email 2 should normally contain:

- One concise sentence, or
- Two short sentences

Avoid unnecessary length.

---

# 12. Directness Check

Email 2 should be direct.

The prospect has already received the initial explanation.

Do not spend the follow-up explaining the same idea from scratch.

---

# 13. Repetition Check

Compare Email 1 and Email 2.

Reject or revise if Email 2:

- Copies Email 1
- Repeats complete sentences
- Uses nearly identical sentence structures
- Repeats the same opening
- Repeats the same wording unnecessarily

The idea should remain consistent.

The expression should be fresh.

---

# 14. Mechanical Paraphrase Check

Do not accept an Email 2 that merely changes a few words from Email 1.

Example:

Email 1:
"Property owners considering roof replacement may need..."

Email 2:
"Property owners looking at roof replacement may need..."

This is too similar.

Rewrite the follow-up from the extracted core proposition.

---

# 15. New-Angle Check

Email 2 should not perform the role assigned to Email 3.

Ask:

"Is this primarily a follow-up to Email 1, or did it introduce a new commercial pitch?"

If it is a new pitch, revise it.

---

# 16. Unrelated-Angle Check

Reject Email 2 if it introduces an unrelated topic.

Example:

Email 1:
Roofing opportunities.

Email 2:
Employee hiring.

This is sequence drift.

---

# 17. Unsupported-Claim Check

Confirm that Email 2 does not introduce unsupported claims about:

- The company
- Its customers
- Its demand
- Its growth
- Its projects
- Its sales
- Its current goals
- Its customer intent

The same evidence rules used for Email 1 apply to Email 2.

---

# 18. New-Fact Check

If Email 2 contains information that was not present in Email 1:

Ask whether that information came from verified lead information or an explicitly supplied update.

If not, remove it.

---

# 19. CTA Check

Confirm:

- CTA is soft
- CTA is concise
- CTA is easy to ignore
- CTA does not demand a meeting
- CTA does not create pressure

Good:

"Worth exploring?"

Avoid:

"Can we schedule 15 minutes?"

---

# 20. Follow-Up Filler Check

Reject unnecessary phrases such as:

- Just following up
- Just checking in
- Circling back
- Touching base
- Wanted to bump this
- Following up on my last email

These phrases do not provide additional value.

---

# 21. Generic Follow-Up Check

Reject:

"Just checking if you're interested."

"Any thoughts?"

"Did you see my last email?"

"Let me know if you're interested."

These messages contain insufficient commercial context.

---

# 22. Tone Check

Email 2 should remain:

- Human
- Professional
- Conversational
- Direct
- Calm
- Low pressure

It should not become more aggressive than Email 1.

---

# 23. AI-Style Check

Avoid:

- Overly polished corporate language
- Repetitive formulaic phrasing
- Artificial transitions
- Excessive adjectives
- Generic sales language

The email should sound manually written.

---

# 24. Hype Check

Reject:

- Game-changing
- Revolutionary
- Massive
- Explosive
- Skyrocket
- Dominate
- 10x
- Supercharge
- Best-in-class
- Incredible

Keep the value understated.

---

# 25. Guarantee Check

Reject claims implying guaranteed:

- Leads
- Meetings
- Customers
- Revenue
- Sales
- Replies
- Conversions
- Pipeline
- Results

Use cautious language.

---

# 26. Deliverability Check

Keep Email 2 clean.

Avoid unnecessary:

- Links
- Images
- Promotional formatting
- Excessive punctuation
- Spam-like language
- Urgency language

Do not add a calendar link.

---

# 27. Continuity Check

Read Email 1 followed by Email 2.

Ask:

"Would the recipient naturally understand Email 2 as a continuation of Email 1?"

If yes, continue.

If no, rewrite.

---

# 28. Meaning Preservation Check

Compare the underlying propositions.

Email 1 proposition:
"Reach property owners considering roofing work."

Email 2 proposition:
"Reach property owners considering roofing work."

The wording can differ.

The meaning should remain consistent.

---

# 29. Over-Compression Check

Do not shorten Email 2 so aggressively that the business context disappears.

Bad:

"Could help create more conversations. Worth exploring?"

Better:

"Could help create more conversations with property owners considering roofing work. Worth exploring?"

---

# 30. Under-Compression Check

Do not let Email 2 become another full explanation.

If Email 2 contains all the information from Email 1, compress it.

---

# 31. One-Idea Check

Email 2 should normally have one primary idea:

The original opportunity is worth another look.

Do not introduce multiple new benefits.

---

# 32. CTA Consistency Check

The CTA should remain consistent with the low-pressure style of Email 1.

Variation is allowed.

Escalation is not.

---

# 33. Recipient Perspective Check

Ask:

"If I received Email 1 and then this Email 2, would I see this as a reasonable follow-up rather than another generic sales message?"

If no, revise.

---

# 34. Final Email 2 Checklist

Before approving:

[ ] Actual Email 1 reviewed

[ ] Core proposition identified

[ ] Business context preserved

[ ] Audience preserved

[ ] Customer situation preserved

[ ] Service preserved

[ ] PersistIQ value preserved

[ ] Message is concise

[ ] Message is direct

[ ] Message is shorter/simpler than Email 1 where practical

[ ] Wording is not copied

[ ] Wording is not a mechanical paraphrase

[ ] No unrelated new angle

[ ] No sequence drift

[ ] No unsupported claims

[ ] No fabricated facts

[ ] No generic follow-up filler

[ ] No "just following up" language

[ ] CTA is soft

[ ] No calendar link

[ ] No unnecessary links

[ ] No images

[ ] No hype

[ ] No guarantees

[ ] No aggressive language

[ ] No artificial urgency

[ ] Human tone

[ ] Natural continuation of Email 1

---

# 35. Final Quality Gate

Email 2 is approved only when it passes the following test:

"Is this a short, direct, natural follow-up to the specific Email 1 that was sent to this prospect?"

If yes, approve.

If no, revise.
