# Email 2 — Rules

## 1. Purpose

Email 2 is a short, concise, direct follow-up to Email 1.

Its purpose is to reinforce the original proposition and give the prospect another easy opportunity to engage.

Email 2 is not a replacement for Email 1.

It is not a second independent pitch.

---

# 2. Core Sequence

The sequence is:

Email 1
→ Initial relevance + PersistIQ value

Email 2
→ Follow-up based on Email 1

Email 3
→ New angle / additional reason to engage

Email 2 must remain between these two roles.

---

# 3. Email 1 Is the Primary Context

When Email 1 is available, Agent-P must read and understand the actual Email 1 before generating Email 2.

Extract:

- Business context
- Audience
- Customer situation
- Service
- PersistIQ value
- CTA
- Key proposition

Email 2 should be based on what was actually communicated.

Do not generate Email 2 solely from the original lead data when Email 1 is available.

---

# 4. Follow-Up, Not Restart

Email 2 should feel like a continuation.

Do not restart with:

- A new introduction
- A new explanation of the business
- A full explanation of PersistIQ
- The complete Email 1 proposition

The prospect has already received Email 1.

---

# 5. Core Proposition

Preserve the central commercial proposition from Email 1.

If Email 1 established:

Business
→ audience
→ situation
→ service
→ relevant prospects
→ PersistIQ value

Email 2 should remain connected to that same proposition.

It may compress or rephrase it.

It should not abandon it.

---

# 6. Concision

Email 2 must be short.

Prefer:

- One or two concise sentences
- Direct wording
- Minimal explanation

Do not add information merely to make the follow-up longer.

The prospect already has the context from Email 1.

---

# 7. Directness

Email 2 should be more direct than Email 1 where natural.

Email 1 establishes the context.

Email 2 can assume that context and focus on the opportunity.

However, directness must not become aggressiveness.

---

# 8. Soft CTA

Email 2 must end with a soft CTA.

The CTA should invite consideration rather than demand action.

Suitable concepts include:

- Worth exploring?
- Open to seeing how?
- Worth a look?
- Open to exploring it?
- Make sense to discuss?
- Would it make sense to explore?

The exact wording should vary naturally.

---

# 9. No New Unrelated Angle

Do not introduce a completely different commercial reason in Email 2.

Example:

Email 1:
Reaching property owners considering roofing work.

Email 2:
Should remain focused on reaching relevant roofing prospects.

Do not suddenly switch to:

- Hiring
- Operations
- Employee retention
- Financing
- Branding

unless the new subject is directly connected to the original proposition and is necessary for the follow-up.

A genuinely new angle belongs primarily in Email 3.

---

# 10. No New Product Proposition

Do not introduce a different PersistIQ benefit simply to make Email 2 different.

The underlying value remains consistent.

Email 2 can rephrase the value.

It should not replace the value with an unrelated product claim.

---

# 11. No Verbatim Copying

Do not copy Email 1 word-for-word.

Avoid repeating:

- The same opening
- The same sentence
- The same phrase sequence
- The same CTA

The message should feel like a follow-up, not a duplicate.

---

# 12. No Mechanical Paraphrasing

Do not simply replace a few words in Email 1.

Bad process:

Email 1:
"Property owners considering roof replacement could be a good fit..."

Email 2:
"Property owners looking at roof replacement could be a good fit..."

This is too close.

Instead, compress the original proposition into a genuinely natural follow-up.

---

# 13. Preserve Business Context

Even though Email 2 is shorter, it should retain enough context to remain understandable.

Do not reduce the message so much that it becomes generic.

Weak:

"Could help you find more prospects. Worth exploring?"

Better:

"Could help create more conversations with property owners considering roofing work. Worth exploring?"

The business context remains visible.

---

# 14. Do Not Over-Explain

Do not repeat all of Email 1.

Avoid:

- Repeating the entire customer situation
- Repeating every service
- Repeating multiple benefits
- Repeating website information
- Explaining PersistIQ again

Choose the central proposition.

---

# 15. Do Not Add Unsupported Claims

Email 2 follows the same evidence rules as Email 1.

Do not invent:

- Current demand
- Customer activity
- Company growth
- Current projects
- Customer intent
- Sales problems
- Company goals

The follow-up must remain grounded in the original evidence and reasoning.

---

# 16. Do Not Increase Sales Pressure

Email 2 should not become aggressive.

Avoid:

- "Can we get 15 minutes?"
- "When are you available?"
- "Let's book a call."
- "You don't want to miss this."
- "Just checking one last time."

Do not use artificial urgency.

---

# 17. No Fake Follow-Up Language

Avoid unnecessary phrases such as:

- Just following up
- Just checking in
- Following up on my last email
- Wanted to bump this
- Bumping this to the top of your inbox
- Circling back
- Touching base

A follow-up should demonstrate value rather than merely announce that it is a follow-up.

---

# 18. Avoid "Did You See My Email?"

Do not use:

- Did you see my email?
- Did you get a chance to read this?
- Did you receive my last message?
- Any thoughts?
- Have you had a chance to look?

These statements add little value.

---

# 19. No Generic Follow-Up

Do not produce:

"Just following up. Let me know if you're interested."

This contains no useful business context or value.

Email 2 should retain the relevant proposition from Email 1.

---

# 20. No Calendar Links

Do not include a calendar link.

The CTA should remain conversational.

---

# 21. No Unnecessary Links

Do not add links simply because Email 2 is a follow-up.

Keep the message clean.

---

# 22. No Images or Promotional Formatting

Keep Email 2 plain and simple.

Avoid:

- Images
- Banners
- Promotional blocks
- Bullets
- Headers
- Decorative formatting

unless explicitly required.

---

# 23. Language

Maintain the Email 1 voice:

- Human
- Simple
- Professional
- Conversational
- Direct
- Low pressure

Avoid:

- Hype
- Corporate jargon
- Marketing clichés
- Artificial personalization
- AI-style language

---

# 24. Avoid AI-Style Follow-Ups

Avoid:

- I wanted to follow up regarding...
- I just wanted to check in...
- I wanted to circle back...
- I thought I'd follow up...
- I hope you're doing well...
- Just checking whether...

The email should communicate the relevant proposition instead.

---

# 25. Repetition Control

Before writing Email 2, identify what has already been said in Email 1.

Email 2 must vary naturally across leads in its opening, sentence structure, and CTA. It must not repeatedly begin with the same construction, including repeated patterns such as "Following up...", "Just following up...", "Checking in...", "Wanted to follow up...", or equivalent repeated constructions.

Changing the customer type, business name, or a few nouns inside the same sentence formula does not constitute meaningful variation. For example, repeatedly using "Would reaching [customer type] [situation] support/fit [business]? Worth [CTA]?" remains a repeated construction even when the variables change.

Variation must occur at the reasoning and sentence-construction level, not through mechanical synonym replacement or a predefined rotation. Evaluate variation across the opening construction, sentence structure, proposition framing, and CTA construction, not merely vocabulary or customer-type substitution. Depending on the lead, Email 2 may use a question-led follow-up, benefit-led follow-up, concise observation or idea, conditional follow-up, direct commercial question, or continuation of the Email 1 proposition from a different grammatical structure. These are construction options, not a fixed rotation or phrase library.

Email 2 must still read the actual Email 1, continue the same core proposition, remain concise and low pressure, avoid a new commercial angle or simple paraphrase, use the actual customer type or relevant context from Email 1 when appropriate, remain commercially relevant to the original offer, use a natural CTA, and contain no em dash character. Do not require artificial uniqueness or force variation when it would make the email less natural; relevance, continuity with Email 1, natural language, and low-pressure delivery take priority.

Do not unnecessarily repeat:

- The same sentence
- The same opening
- The same service description
- The same value wording
- The same CTA

Preserve the idea, not the wording.

---

# 26. One Primary Idea

Email 2 should normally contain one central idea:

The original opportunity is still relevant.

Do not introduce multiple new benefits.

Do not turn Email 2 into a second sales presentation.

---

# 27. Appropriate Compression

Email 2 should compress Email 1.

The agent should identify:

What is the shortest natural way to remind the prospect of the original proposition?

Then write that message.

Compression does not mean deleting all business context.

It means removing unnecessary explanation.

---

# 28. Business-Specific Follow-Up

The follow-up should still reflect the business.

Example:

Email 1:
Property owners with aging roofs may be considering inspections or replacement.

Email 2:
Could help create more conversations with property owners considering roofing work. Worth exploring?

This retains the commercial context without repeating the entire first email.

---

# 29. CTA Variation

CTA variation should be natural.

Do not force a different CTA every time.

If the same CTA is the most natural choice, it may be reused occasionally.

Avoid creating unnatural CTA variations merely for uniqueness.

---

# 30. Tone Consistency

Email 2 should sound like the same sender as Email 1.

Maintain consistency in:

- Confidence
- Simplicity
- Professionalism
- Softness
- Commercial awareness

The tone should not suddenly become:

- More enthusiastic
- More aggressive
- More promotional
- More casual

---

# 31. No New Facts

Email 2 should not introduce new company-specific facts unless new verified information is explicitly provided as part of the input.

The follow-up should primarily work with the established proposition.

---

# 32. No Sequence Drift

Sequence drift occurs when Email 2 gradually changes the original proposition into a different pitch.

Avoid this.

The sequence should remain:

Email 1 proposition
→ Email 2 reinforcement
→ Email 3 new relevant angle

---

# 33. Recipient Test

Ask:

"If I received Email 1 and then received this Email 2, would this feel like a natural follow-up?"

If no, revise.

---

# 34. Value Test

Ask:

"Does Email 2 still communicate why this opportunity may be relevant?"

If no, it has been compressed too far.

---

# 35. Brevity Test

Ask:

"Can any sentence or phrase be removed without losing the central proposition?"

If yes, remove it.

---

# 36. Final Email 2 Checklist

Before approving Email 2:

[ ] Actual Email 1 was reviewed

[ ] Core proposition was identified

[ ] Business context is preserved

[ ] Audience remains relevant

[ ] Service remains relevant

[ ] PersistIQ value remains consistent

[ ] Email is short

[ ] Email is concise

[ ] Email is direct

[ ] CTA is soft

[ ] No unrelated new angle

[ ] No new unsupported claims

[ ] No verbatim copying

[ ] No mechanical paraphrasing

[ ] No generic follow-up language

[ ] No "just following up" filler

[ ] No calendar link

[ ] No unnecessary links

[ ] No hype

[ ] No aggressive language

[ ] No artificial urgency

[ ] No AI-style follow-up language

[ ] Natural continuation of Email 1

---

# 37. Final Rule

Email 2 should answer:

"What is the shortest, most natural way to follow up on the specific proposition I already presented in Email 1?"

Do that without restarting the pitch, changing the proposition, or pressuring the prospect.
