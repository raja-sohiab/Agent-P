# Email 2 — Sequencing Logic

## 1. Purpose

This document defines how Agent-P should move from Email 1 to Email 2.

Email 2 must be generated with awareness of the message that was already sent.

The goal is continuity without repetition.

---

# 2. Sequence Position

The current sequence is:

Email 1
→ Initial relevance + value

Email 2
→ Follow-up based on Email 1

Email 3
→ New angle / additional reason to engage

Email 2 is therefore a continuation stage.

---

# 3. Required Input

When generating Email 2, Agent-P should have:

1. Lead information
2. Business understanding
3. Actual Email 1
4. Email 2 rules
5. PersistIQ value knowledge
6. Sequence logic

The actual Email 1 is the most important sequence input.

---

# 4. Read Email 1 First

Before writing Email 2:

1. Read the complete Email 1.
2. Identify its central proposition.
3. Identify the audience.
4. Identify the customer situation.
5. Identify the relevant service.
6. Identify the PersistIQ value.
7. Identify the CTA.
8. Remove secondary information.
9. Build a concise follow-up around the central proposition.

Do not write Email 2 before understanding Email 1.

---

# 5. Extract the Core Proposition

Reduce Email 1 to one short internal statement.

Example:

Email 1:

"Businesses outgrowing basic bookkeeping may need more structured accounting support. We could help create more conversations with owners considering that support."

Core proposition:

"Reach business owners considering accounting support."

Email 2 should be built from this proposition.

---

# 6. Preserve the Business

The business context from Email 1 must remain intact.

Do not change the company interpretation between Email 1 and Email 2.

If Email 1 is about:

- Roofing

Email 2 should remain about roofing.

If Email 1 is about:

- Accounting

Email 2 should remain about accounting.

If Email 1 is about:

- Software development

Email 2 should remain about software development.

---

# 7. Preserve the Audience

Keep the relevant audience consistent unless Email 1 itself used an overly broad audience that can be safely clarified.

Examples:

Email 1:
Property owners considering roofing work.

Email 2:
Property owners considering roofing work.

Not:

Email 2:
Software companies.

---

# 8. Preserve the Commercial Situation

The central customer situation should remain recognizable.

Examples:

Email 1:
Businesses evaluating automation.

Email 2:
Businesses exploring automation.

Email 1:
Property owners planning renovations.

Email 2:
Property owners considering renovation work.

The wording may change.

The underlying situation should remain.

---

# 9. Preserve the Service Connection

Email 2 must remain connected to the service introduced in Email 1.

Do not remove the service context entirely if doing so makes the message generic.

The prospect should still understand what type of opportunity is being discussed.

---

# 10. Preserve the PersistIQ Value

The value proposition should remain consistent.

If Email 1 says:

"We could help create more conversations with businesses considering..."

Email 2 may say:

"Could help reach more businesses considering..."

The wording changes.

The value remains.

---

# 11. Compress Secondary Information

Email 1 may contain several details.

Email 2 should normally retain only the strongest one.

Example:

Email 1 contains:

- Audience
- Problem
- Three services
- Multiple customer situations
- PersistIQ value

Email 2 should normally focus on:

- Strongest audience
- Strongest situation
- Core service
- PersistIQ value
- CTA

---

# 12. Remove Redundancy

Do not carry every sentence from Email 1 into Email 2.

Remove:

- Repeated explanations
- Supporting details
- Secondary services
- Extra qualifiers
- Website details
- Repeated descriptions

Keep the commercial core.

---

# 13. Rephrase, Do Not Copy

Email 2 should communicate the same core proposition using fresh language.

Example:

Email 1:
"Homeowners planning renovations often look for a designer who can turn ideas into a cohesive space. We could help create more conversations with people considering that guidance."

Email 2:
"Could help reach more homeowners considering renovation design. Worth exploring?"

The meaning is preserved.

The wording is different.

---

# 14. CTA Continuity

Email 2 should remain consistent with the soft CTA style of Email 1.

The CTA may:

- Stay the same
- Be lightly rephrased
- Use another approved soft CTA

Do not escalate the CTA.

---

# 15. No New Angle Yet

Email 2 should not perform the Email 3 job.

The main Email 2 task is:

Follow up on the original proposition.

Do not introduce an unrelated customer segment or commercial problem.

---

# 16. No Sequence Drift

Sequence drift occurs when Email 2 gradually becomes a different pitch.

Example:

Email 1:
Reach homeowners considering roofing services.

Email 2:
Reach homeowners considering roofing services.

Correct.

Example:

Email 1:
Reach homeowners considering roofing services.

Email 2:
Your sales team may need better CRM reporting.

Incorrect.

---

# 17. No New Unsupported Information

Email 2 must not introduce a new company-specific fact that was not established in Email 1 or the verified lead information.

A follow-up is not an opportunity to invent additional personalization.

---

# 18. New Information Provided Later

If verified new information is explicitly provided between Email 1 and Email 2, it may be considered.

However, new information should only be used if it is:

- Relevant
- Accurate
- Commercially useful
- Consistent with the sequence

Do not introduce new information merely to create variation.

---

# 19. Email 1 Should Remain the Anchor

When there is a conflict between:

- A new creative idea
- The original Email 1 proposition

Email 1 should normally remain the anchor.

Email 2 is a follow-up.

Email 3 is where the sequence gains a new angle.

---

# 20. Sequence State

Agent-P should conceptually maintain the following state:

### Business

What does the company do?

### Audience

Who is relevant?

### Situation

What customer situation was identified?

### Service

What service is relevant?

### Value

How does PersistIQ connect?

### Email 1

What was actually communicated?

### Email 2

What has now been communicated?

This sequence state becomes important when Email 3 is generated.

---

# 21. Email 2 Does Not Need to Mention Email 1

The email itself does not need to say:

- "As mentioned in my first email"
- "Following up on my last email"
- "As I said previously"

The sequence connection should come from the proposition.

---

# 22. Recipient Experience

When Email 1 and Email 2 are read together, the recipient should feel:

Email 1:
"This may be relevant to my business."

Email 2:
"This is a concise follow-up on that same idea."

Not:

Email 1:
"One sales pitch."

Email 2:
"Another unrelated sales pitch."

---

# 23. Compression Test

After generating Email 2, compare it with Email 1.

Ask:

- Is the core idea preserved?
- Is unnecessary detail removed?
- Is the message shorter?
- Is the wording fresh?
- Is the value still clear?

If yes, sequencing is working.

---

# 24. Continuity Test

Read Email 1 followed immediately by Email 2.

Ask:

"Does Email 2 logically follow Email 1?"

If no:

- Re-read Email 1
- Re-extract the proposition
- Rebuild Email 2

---

# 25. Final Sequencing Process

The required process is:

Lead
↓
Understand business
↓
Email 1
↓
Extract Email 1 proposition
↓
Preserve business + audience + situation + service
↓
Compress
↓
Rephrase
↓
Reinforce PersistIQ value
↓
Add soft CTA
↓
QA
↓
Email 2

---

# 26. Final Rule

Email 2 should remember what was already said.

It should say less, not start over.

The objective is:

Continuity
+
Compression
+
Reinforcement
+
Soft CTA

Not:

Repetition
+
New pitch
+
More sales pressure
