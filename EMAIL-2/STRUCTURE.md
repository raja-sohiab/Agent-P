# Email 2 — Structure

## 1. Purpose

Email 2 is a concise follow-up built from the actual Email 1 generated for the lead.

The structure should compress the original proposition while preserving:

- Business context
- Relevant audience
- Core opportunity
- PersistIQ value
- Soft CTA

Email 2 should feel like a natural continuation of Email 1.

---

# 2. Core Structure

The underlying structure is:

Email 1 Core Proposition
↓
Compressed Relevance
↓
PersistIQ Value
↓
Soft CTA

The structure is flexible.

It is not a fixed sentence template.

---

# 3. Source Material

The primary source for Email 2 is the actual Email 1.

Agent-P should identify:

- The main business situation
- The relevant audience
- The service
- The opportunity
- The PersistIQ value

Then compress those elements into a short follow-up.

---

# 4. Core Proposition Extraction

Before writing Email 2, reduce Email 1 to one internal proposition.

Example:

Email 1:

"Property owners facing aging or storm-damaged roofs may need the inspections and replacements you provide. We could help surface more nearby projects worth discussing."

Core proposition:

"Reach property owners considering roofing work."

Email 2 should be built around that proposition.

---

# 5. Compression

Remove information that the prospect already received.

Email 2 generally does not need:

- Full business explanation
- Full customer situation
- Multiple service details
- Extensive context
- Product explanation

Keep the most commercially useful part.

---

# 6. Business Context

Email 2 should retain enough context to remind the prospect what the opportunity concerns.

Example:

"Could help create more conversations with property owners considering roofing work."

This is preferable to:

"Could help create more conversations."

The second version loses too much context.

---

# 7. PersistIQ Value

The value should remain connected to the original proposition.

Possible value concepts:

- Create more conversations
- Reach relevant prospects
- Surface relevant opportunities
- Connect with potential customers
- Reach businesses considering the service

Use the value concept that best matches Email 1.

---

# 8. CTA

End with a soft CTA.

Examples:

- Worth exploring?
- Worth a look?
- Open to seeing how?
- Open to exploring it?
- Make sense to discuss?

The CTA should feel like a natural continuation of the message.

---

# 9. Preferred Length

Email 2 should normally be:

- One sentence, or
- Two very short sentences

One sentence is preferred when the complete proposition can be communicated naturally.

Do not add a second sentence simply to satisfy a format.

---

# 10. One-Sentence Structure

When possible:

Core opportunity
+
PersistIQ value
+
Soft CTA

Conceptually:

"Could help create more conversations with [relevant audience] considering [service]. Worth exploring?"

Do not use this as a fixed template.

---

# 11. Two-Sentence Structure

When one sentence becomes awkward:

Sentence 1:
Short reminder of the original opportunity.

Sentence 2:
PersistIQ value + soft CTA.

Conceptually:

"[Relevant audience] considering [situation] may still be a useful audience to reach.

We could help create more of those conversations. Worth exploring?"

Again, write naturally rather than mechanically.

---

# 12. Follow-Up Framing

The follow-up does not need to explicitly announce that it is a follow-up.

Avoid:

"Just following up on my last email."

Instead, demonstrate continuation through the proposition itself.

---

# 13. No Full Restatement

Do not reproduce Email 1 in shorter words.

Compression means identifying the central idea and expressing it independently.

Example:

Email 1:
"Property owners dealing with aging roofs often compare inspection, repair, and replacement options. We could help create more conversations with people considering that work."

Bad Email 2:

"Property owners dealing with aging roofs often compare inspection, repair, and replacement options. We could help create more conversations with people considering that work. Worth exploring?"

This is effectively Email 1 plus a CTA.

Better:

"Could help create more conversations with property owners considering roofing work. Worth exploring?"

---

# 14. No New Commercial Angle

Email 2 should not introduce the new-angle strategy reserved for Email 3.

Example:

Email 1:
Roofing opportunities

Email 2:
More roofing opportunities

Email 3:
Another relevant roofing-related opportunity

Email 2 should stay close to the original proposition.

---

# 15. Maintain Service Context

The follow-up should not become so abstract that the service disappears.

Weak:

"Could help you reach more prospects. Worth exploring?"

Better:

"Could help reach more businesses considering your accounting services. Worth exploring?"

The service or its commercial context should remain understandable.

---

# 16. Maintain Audience Context

Where practical, retain the relevant audience.

Examples:

- Property owners
- Business owners
- Manufacturers
- Homeowners
- Buyers
- Investors
- Businesses
- Organizations

The audience should be specific enough to preserve relevance.

---

# 17. Do Not Add Unnecessary Details

Email 2 should normally remove secondary details from Email 1.

If Email 1 mentions:

- Three services
- Two audiences
- Several situations

Email 2 should normally select the strongest central proposition rather than repeat everything.

---

# 18. Natural Rephrasing

Email 2 should use fresh wording while preserving meaning.

Example:

Email 1 concept:

"Businesses evaluating automation may be considering outside technical support."

Email 2 concept:

"Could help reach companies exploring automation support."

The wording changes.

The commercial idea remains.

---

# 19. Preserve Meaning

Do not rephrase Email 1 so aggressively that the proposition changes.

The following must remain logically consistent:

Audience
+
Need
+
Service
+
PersistIQ opportunity

---

# 20. CTA Placement

The CTA should normally appear at the end.

Preferred:

"Could help reach businesses considering your service. Worth exploring?"

Avoid placing the CTA in the middle of the proposition.

---

# 21. No Explanation of PersistIQ

Email 2 does not need to explain how PersistIQ works.

The prospect only needs to understand the relevant outcome/value.

---

# 22. Business-Specific Adaptation

The structure should adapt to the original Email 1.

Examples:

### Professional service

"Could help create more conversations with business owners considering accounting support. Worth exploring?"

### Home service

"Could help reach more property owners considering roofing work. Worth a look?"

### Technology

"Could help connect with businesses evaluating automation solutions. Open to seeing how?"

### Financial service

"Could help create more conversations with people considering financial planning. Worth exploring?"

These are structural examples, not fixed templates.

---

# 23. Do Not Copy Reference Examples

The 501 Email 1 examples are not Email 2 templates.

Email 2 must be generated from:

1. The current lead
2. The actual Email 1
3. Email 2 rules
4. Sequence logic

Reference examples may inform general language quality but should not be copied.

---

# 24. Sequence Continuity

Email 2 should feel connected to Email 1 when the two are read together.

Example:

Email 1:
"Property owners planning exterior upgrades often need a roofing team for the work. We could help create more conversations with people considering those projects."

Email 2:
"Could help reach more property owners considering exterior work. Worth exploring?"

The second email clearly continues the first.

---

# 25. Avoid Artificial Continuity

Do not force continuity with phrases such as:

- As mentioned above
- As I said
- As noted in my previous email
- Following up on the above
- Per my last email

Continuity should come from the message itself.

---

# 26. Sequence Memory Requirement

When Email 2 is generated, the agent must use the actual Email 1 as sequence context.

The generation process is:

Lead
↓
Understand business
↓
Generate Email 1
↓
Extract Email 1 core proposition
↓
Compress proposition
↓
Write Email 2
↓
QA

Do not skip the Email 1 proposition extraction step.

---

# 27. If Email 1 Is Missing

If the task requires Email 2 but the actual Email 1 is unavailable:

Do not invent what Email 1 supposedly said.

Use the lead information only if the system explicitly permits fallback generation.

If fallback generation is permitted, construct a minimal proposition from the lead and clearly treat it as a fallback rather than pretending to follow a message that was never provided.

---

# 28. Final Structure Test

Before approving Email 2, verify:

### Source

Was the actual Email 1 used?

### Compression

Is the message shorter and more direct?

### Context

Does enough business context remain?

### Value

Is the PersistIQ value still present?

### Continuity

Does it naturally follow Email 1?

### CTA

Is the CTA soft?

### Angle

Is it still the Email 1 proposition rather than the new-angle strategy?

---

# 29. Core Formula

Email 2:

SHORTER
+
DIRECTER
+
SAME CORE PROPOSITION
+
SAME PERSISTIQ VALUE
+
SOFT CTA

Email 2 should feel like a follow-up, not a rewritten Email 1.
