# Email 3 — Patterns

## 1. Purpose

Patterns provide reasoning models for finding a new commercial angle.

They are not templates.

The agent must adapt the pattern to the actual business, service, audience, and sequence context.

---

# 2. Pattern Selection

Select the pattern based on:

- What the business provides
- Who may need the service
- What situation creates demand
- What angle was already used
- What additional legitimate opportunity remains

Do not force a pattern when another reasoning approach is more appropriate.

---

# 3. Audience Expansion

### Logic

Existing audience
→ Different but relevant audience
→ Same service
→ PersistIQ value

Use when the company serves multiple legitimate customer groups.

### Example

Email 1:

Residential property owners may need roofing services.

Email 3:

Commercial property owners may also require roofing support.

The audience changes, but the service relationship remains valid.

---

# 4. Situation Expansion

### Logic

Existing customer situation
→ Another customer situation
→ Same service
→ PersistIQ value

Use when multiple situations can naturally create demand.

### Example

Email 1:

Property owners dealing with storm damage.

Email 3:

Property owners planning broader exterior improvements.

Both situations can create legitimate roofing opportunities.

---

# 5. Trigger Expansion

### Logic

Existing commercial trigger
→ Different relevant trigger
→ Service need
→ PersistIQ value

Possible triggers include:

- Replacement
- Expansion
- Planning
- Maintenance
- Renovation
- Evaluation
- New projects
- Operational change

Only use triggers that are reasonable for the business.

---

# 6. Service-Need Expansion

### Logic

Existing service need
→ Another legitimate need connected to the service
→ PersistIQ value

Use when a company provides multiple related services or solves several related customer problems.

Do not invent services that the company does not provide.

---

# 7. Buying-Decision Pattern

### Logic

Customer evaluating one need
→ Another relevant decision point
→ Company's service
→ PersistIQ value

Useful for businesses whose customers typically research, compare, plan, or evaluate before purchasing.

Examples:

- Selecting a provider
- Comparing options
- Planning a project
- Evaluating replacement
- Looking for specialist support

---

# 8. Planning-to-Provider Pattern

### Logic

Customers planning an activity
→ Company's service becomes relevant
→ PersistIQ can help reach those prospects

Example:

Businesses planning facility improvements may need specialized contractors.

The company could use outbound conversations to reach businesses considering those projects.

---

# 9. Replacement Pattern

### Logic

Existing or aging solution
→ Replacement decision
→ Company's service
→ PersistIQ value

Use for businesses where replacement naturally creates demand.

Examples:

- Equipment
- Roofing
- Software
- Professional services
- Industrial systems
- Property services

Do not claim a prospect actually needs replacement unless supported.

Use cautious language.

---

# 10. Maintenance Pattern

### Logic

Ongoing ownership/use
→ Maintenance requirement
→ Company's service
→ PersistIQ value

Useful for businesses providing:

- Maintenance
- Repairs
- Inspections
- Support
- Recurring services

The need must be commercially plausible.

---

# 11. Growth Pattern

### Logic

Business growth or expansion context
→ New requirement
→ Company's service
→ PersistIQ value

Use carefully.

Do not claim that the specific prospect's company is growing unless there is evidence.

It is safer to refer to:

- Businesses expanding
- Companies adding locations
- Organizations taking on new projects
- Businesses entering new stages

when such situations are commercially relevant.

---

# 12. Evaluation Pattern

### Logic

Potential customer evaluating a solution
→ Company's service
→ PersistIQ value

Useful for businesses whose customers commonly research or compare providers before making a decision.

Examples:

- Professional services
- Technology consulting
- Financial services
- Contractors
- Agencies
- Specialized B2B services

---

# 13. Project Pattern

### Logic

Relevant project
→ Company's service becomes necessary or useful
→ PersistIQ value

Use when the company provides project-based services.

Possible projects:

- Construction
- Renovation
- Development
- Implementation
- Installation
- Modernization

Do not invent specific projects.

---

# 14. Adjacent-Service Pattern

### Logic

Existing service
→ Closely related service need
→ Same business can address it
→ PersistIQ value

Only use when the business actually offers the related service.

Do not expand the company's capabilities beyond available evidence.

---

# 15. Prospect-Need Pattern

### Logic

Relevant prospect problem
→ Company's service addresses the problem
→ PersistIQ can help reach those prospects

This pattern focuses on the prospect's likely need rather than the company's internal characteristics.

Use cautious language when the need is inferred.

---

# 16. Decision-Moment Pattern

### Logic

Customer reaches a decision point
→ Company's service becomes relevant
→ PersistIQ can help surface conversations

Decision points may include:

- Choosing a provider
- Replacing a solution
- Starting a project
- Planning an upgrade
- Evaluating alternatives
- Preparing for a new requirement

---

# 17. Cross-Segment Pattern

### Logic

Existing customer segment
→ Another legitimate customer segment
→ Same service
→ PersistIQ value

Use only when the company genuinely serves multiple segments.

Do not use ICP qualification.

The question is:

"Does this company actually serve this segment?"

Not:

"Is this segment part of the ICP?"

---

# 18. Commercial Opportunity Pattern

### Logic

Another plausible source of demand
→ Company's service
→ More relevant conversations
→ PersistIQ

This is useful when no obvious second customer situation exists but another legitimate commercial opportunity can be identified.

---

# 19. Pattern Combination

Patterns may be combined when necessary.

Example:

**Different audience**
+
**Different customer situation**
→
**Same service**
→
**PersistIQ value**

However, keep the final email focused on one primary commercial idea.

---

# 20. Pattern Restrictions

Patterns must never be used to justify:

- Fabricated facts
- Fake personalization
- Unsupported customer intent
- Unsupported company growth
- Unsupported projects
- Unsupported demand
- Unsupported problems
- Unsupported capabilities

A pattern is a reasoning aid, not evidence.

---

# 21. Choosing the Best Pattern

Prefer the pattern that:

1. Creates a genuinely new angle
2. Has the strongest evidence
3. Has the clearest service connection
4. Makes commercial sense
5. Can be expressed concisely
6. Does not repeat Email 1 or Email 2

---

# 22. Pattern Example

Suppose a financial planning firm serves:

- Individuals
- Families
- Business owners

Email 1 focuses on:

**Individuals planning their finances.**

Email 3 could use:

**Business owners evaluating financial planning needs.**

The angle is different.

The business remains the same.

The service remains relevant.

PersistIQ's value remains consistent.

---

# 23. Pattern Example — Professional Services

Email 1:

Businesses needing ongoing accounting support.

Email 3:

Business owners approaching a transition or planning event that creates a need for financial guidance.

The second message introduces a different commercial situation rather than repeating the original one.

---

# 24. Pattern Example — Technology Services

Email 1:

Companies dealing with a specific operational challenge.

Email 3:

Companies planning modernization or evaluating a related technology improvement.

Both remain connected to the company's actual technology services.

---

# 25. Pattern Example — Contractors

Email 1:

Property owners needing repairs.

Email 3:

Property owners planning renovations or new projects.

The commercial trigger changes while the service relationship remains intact.

---

# 26. Avoid Mechanical Rotation

Do not rotate patterns in a fixed order.

For example:

Email 3 = Audience Expansion
Email 3 = Situation Expansion
Email 3 = Replacement
Email 3 = Maintenance

This creates artificial behavior.

Select the pattern based on the lead.

---

# 27. Do Not Force Multiple Angles

Do not use several patterns simply to make the email appear sophisticated.

One strong angle is better than several weak ones.

---

# 28. Final Pattern Principle

Patterns answer:

**"What legitimate additional commercial angle could this business be approached around?"**

They do not answer:

**"What different wording can I use for the same pitch?"**
