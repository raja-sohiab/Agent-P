# Email 3 — Purpose

## 1. Role

Email 3 is the third message in the PersistIQ outbound sequence.

Its purpose is to introduce a new, relevant commercial angle or an additional reason for the prospect to consider engaging.

Email 3 should build on the business understanding established earlier in the sequence without simply repeating Email 1 or Email 2.

---

# 2. Sequence Position

The sequence is:

Email 1
→ Initial relevance + value

Email 2
→ Follow-up based on Email 1

Email 3
→ New angle / additional reason to engage

Email 3 is therefore the first stage where a genuinely different commercial angle may be introduced.

---

# 3. Primary Objective

Email 3 should:

- Introduce another legitimate reason the business may benefit from outbound prospecting
- Remain relevant to the same business
- Connect the new angle to the company's actual service
- Connect the opportunity to PersistIQ
- Use a soft CTA
- Remain short and direct

---

# 4. New Angle

A new angle means a different but related way of thinking about the business's commercial opportunity.

Possible sources include:

- Another customer situation
- Another relevant audience
- Another commercial trigger
- Another service-related need
- Another type of prospect
- Another reason prospects may need the company's service

The new angle must remain logically connected to the business.

---

# 5. New Angle Does Not Mean New Business

The business does not change.

If Email 1 was about:

Roofing

Email 3 must still relate to the roofing business.

The new angle could involve:

- Property owners considering replacement
- Property owners planning broader exterior work
- Commercial properties requiring ongoing maintenance

It should not suddenly become about an unrelated business problem.

---

# 6. New Angle Does Not Mean New Product

PersistIQ remains the product.

Do not introduce:

- Another product
- Another campaign
- Another product's value proposition
- Another company's positioning

The angle changes.

The product remains consistent.

---

# 7. Relationship to Email 1

Email 1 establishes the first commercial proposition.

Email 3 should understand that proposition before selecting a new angle.

Example:

Email 1:

Property owners considering roof replacement
→ roofing service
→ PersistIQ

Email 3 may explore:

Property owners planning broader exterior improvements
→ roofing may become part of that project
→ PersistIQ

The second angle is different but related.

---

# 8. Relationship to Email 2

Email 2 follows up on Email 1.

Email 3 should recognize what has already been communicated and avoid simply repeating it.

The sequence should progress:

Email 1:
Initial proposition

Email 2:
Follow-up

Email 3:
Additional proposition

---

# 9. Commercial Relevance

The new angle must have a real commercial connection.

Ask:

"Why would this additional situation naturally create a need for this company's service?"

If there is no clear answer, do not use the angle.

---

# 10. Angle Selection

When selecting a new angle, consider:

1. What does the business actually provide?
2. Who else may need that service?
3. What other situations can create demand for it?
4. What relevant customer decision may occur?
5. Can that opportunity logically be reached through outbound prospecting?
6. Can PersistIQ naturally support that opportunity?

Choose the strongest legitimate angle.

---

# 11. Do Not Force a New Angle

Email 3 must not create novelty simply for the sake of being different.

A weak new angle is worse than a closely related reinforcement.

If no strong alternative angle exists, use the closest valid commercial extension of the original proposition.

---

# 12. Evidence

The new angle must follow the same evidence rules as Email 1 and Email 2.

Use:

- Explicit business information
- Strong category-level knowledge
- Reasonable commercial inference

Do not invent company-specific facts.

---

# 13. Cautious Language

When the new angle is inferred rather than known, use appropriate language.

Examples:

- may
- might
- could
- often
- businesses considering
- companies evaluating
- people looking for
- customers planning

Avoid presenting inferred customer situations as known facts.

---

# 14. Concision

Email 3 should be short.

The prospect has already received two messages.

Do not provide a long explanation.

Prefer:

- One or two short sentences
- One commercial idea
- One PersistIQ value connection
- One soft CTA

---

# 15. Directness

Email 3 can be direct because the prospect has already received the earlier messages.

Avoid unnecessary introductions.

Do not explain the entire business again.

Go directly to the additional relevant reason.

---

# 16. Soft CTA

Email 3 should end with a soft CTA.

Examples:

- Worth exploring?
- Worth a look?
- Open to seeing how?
- Open to exploring it?
- Make sense to discuss?
- Would it make sense to explore?

The CTA should not become more aggressive simply because this is Email 3.

---

# 17. No Artificial Escalation

Email 3 should not use:

- Last chance
- Final attempt
- Don't miss out
- Act now
- Can we book a call?
- When are you free?
- You need to act

The sequence remains low pressure.

---

# 18. No Generic Final Follow-Up

Avoid:

"Just checking one last time."

"Any thoughts?"

"Let me know if you're interested."

"Should I close the loop?"

These do not provide a meaningful additional reason to engage.

---

# 19. No Random New Topic

The new angle must relate to the company's commercial reality.

Do not introduce unrelated topics such as:

- Hiring
- Employee retention
- Internal culture
- Unrelated technology
- Unrelated marketing services

unless the new topic is directly relevant to the company's service and commercial opportunity.

---

# 20. Preserve Product Value

The PersistIQ value should remain conceptually consistent.

Possible value expressions:

- Create more conversations
- Reach relevant prospects
- Surface relevant opportunities
- Connect with potential customers
- Reach businesses considering the service

Choose the value expression that naturally fits the new angle.

---

# 21. New Angle vs New Value

These are different.

### New angle

A different reason the business may want to reach prospects.

### New value

A different explanation of what PersistIQ does.

Email 3 should primarily introduce a new angle.

It does not need a new product value proposition.

---

# 22. Avoid Repeating Email 1

Do not rewrite Email 1 with different words.

If Email 1 already focuses on one customer situation, Email 3 should look for another relevant situation when one exists.

---

# 23. Avoid Repeating Email 2

Email 3 should not simply continue the same follow-up wording.

Email 2 reinforces.

Email 3 expands the commercial conversation with another relevant reason.

---

# 24. Sequence Progression

The intended progression is:

### Email 1

"Here is one reason this could be relevant."

### Email 2

"Following up on that reason."

### Email 3

"Here is another reason this could be relevant."

This should feel like natural progression.

---

# 25. Recipient Experience

After receiving all three messages, the prospect should understand:

1. The sender understands the type of business.
2. There is a legitimate commercial opportunity.
3. The sender is not simply repeating the same pitch.
4. There may be more than one relevant reason to explore the opportunity.

---

# 26. No Pressure Escalation

The sequence should not become:

Email 1:
Soft

Email 2:
Stronger

Email 3:
Aggressive

Instead:

Email 1:
Initial relevance

Email 2:
Follow-up

Email 3:
Additional relevance

Tone remains consistent.

---

# 27. Final Purpose

Email 3 should answer:

"What is another legitimate, business-relevant reason this prospect might benefit from reaching relevant prospects through PersistIQ?"

Answer that question briefly.

Then end with a soft CTA.
