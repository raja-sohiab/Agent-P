# Email 3 — QA

## 1. Purpose

Email 3 must pass all quality checks before it is returned.

QA must verify:

- New-angle validity
- Sequence continuity
- Business relevance
- Service relevance
- PersistIQ connection
- Evidence quality
- Concision
- Natural language
- Deliverability
- Originality
- CTA quality

---

# 2. New Angle Check

Ask:

> Is Email 3 introducing a genuinely different commercial angle?

Pass if:

- The customer situation is different
- The audience is meaningfully different
- The commercial trigger is different
- The service-related need is different
- The commercial opportunity is meaningfully different

Fail if Email 3 is simply a rewritten version of Email 1 or Email 2.

---

# 3. Email 1 Comparison

Compare Email 3 directly against Email 1.

Check:

- Audience
- Situation
- Trigger
- Need
- Commercial proposition
- Core wording

Email 3 may share the same business and service.

It should not repeat the same primary commercial idea.

---

# 4. Email 2 Comparison

Compare Email 3 against Email 2.

Email 2 is a follow-up.

Email 3 should introduce additional relevance rather than another generic follow-up.

Fail if Email 3 mainly says:

- Just following up
- Checking in
- Circling back
- Any thoughts
- Wanted to bump this

without adding a meaningful new commercial angle.

---

# 5. Business Understanding

Verify that Email 3 accurately represents the business.

Check:

- Company type
- Service
- Relevant customers
- Commercial context

Do not introduce capabilities or services the company does not provide.

---

# 6. Service Connection

Ask:

> Does the new angle naturally create a reason to use this company's service?

If the connection is weak or artificial, reject the email.

---

# 7. PersistIQ Connection

Ask:

> Does the email naturally explain how PersistIQ could help with this additional opportunity?

The connection should be about:

- Relevant outbound conversations
- Relevant prospects
- Potential customers
- Commercial opportunities

Do not turn the email into a generic PersistIQ product pitch.

---

# 8. Evidence Check

Classify the main claims:

### Level 1 — Explicit

Directly supported by available business information.

**Allowed.**

### Level 2 — Strong category inference

A highly plausible relationship based on the business/service.

**Allowed with natural wording.**

### Level 3 — Reasonable contextual inference

Plausible but less certain.

**Allowed with cautious wording.**

### Level 4 — Unsupported company-specific assumption

Not supported by available information.

**Reject.**

---

# 9. Unsupported Claims

Reject claims suggesting that the company:

- Is currently growing
- Is expanding
- Has a specific problem
- Has specific customers
- Is looking for customers
- Has specific projects
- Is losing customers
- Is entering a market
- Is changing its strategy

unless supported by evidence.

---

# 10. Inference Language

When an idea is inferred, verify appropriate wording.

Preferred:

- may
- might
- could
- often
- businesses considering
- companies evaluating
- people looking for
- customers planning

Avoid presenting assumptions as facts.

---

# 11. One Primary Angle

Email 3 should have one main commercial idea.

Reject emails that contain several unrelated opportunities.

The reader should be able to summarize the new angle in one short sentence.

---

# 12. Sequence Progression

Verify:

**Email 1**
→ Initial relevance + value

**Email 2**
→ Follow-up based on Email 1

**Email 3**
→ New angle / additional reason

The progression should be obvious.

---

# 13. No Sequence Reset

Reject if Email 3:

- Reintroduces the entire company
- Repeats Email 1
- Repeats Email 2
- Starts a completely unrelated pitch

It must feel like the third message in an existing sequence.

---

# 14. Concision Check

Prefer:

- One or two sentences
- One primary idea
- One PersistIQ connection
- One soft CTA

Remove unnecessary explanation.

---

# 15. Naturalness Check

Read the email as if you were the recipient.

Ask:

> Does this sound like a real person sending a short third outbound email?

Reject if it feels:

- Robotic
- Overwritten
- Formulaic
- Artificially personalized
- Overly polished
- Sales-heavy

---

# 16. Language Check

Use:

- Simple words
- Short sentences
- Natural phrasing
- Professional conversational tone
- Direct language

Avoid:

- Corporate jargon
- Marketing clichés
- Unnecessary adjectives
- Overly complex sentences

---

# 17. Prohibited Openers

Do not begin with:

- I noticed
- I came across
- I saw that
- I was impressed by
- As a leading
- In today's world

Use direct relevance instead.

---

# 18. Hype Check

Reject language such as:

- Game-changing
- Revolutionary
- Guaranteed
- Explosive growth
- Skyrocket
- Dominate
- Massive
- Incredible
- Cutting-edge
- Best-in-class
- Unlock
- 10x
- Supercharge

Keep the message credible.

---

# 19. Guarantee Check

Never guarantee:

- Leads
- Meetings
- Revenue
- Customers
- Growth
- Responses
- Results

Use possibility-based language.

---

# 20. CTA Check

The CTA must be:

- Short
- Soft
- Natural
- Low pressure

Good examples:

- Worth exploring?
- Worth a look?
- Open to exploring it?
- Make sense to discuss?
- Open to seeing how?

Reject aggressive meeting requests.

---

# 21. Generic Follow-Up Check

Reject if the email could be sent to almost any company without changing its meaning.

For example:

> Just wanted to follow up and see if you're interested in getting more leads.

This contains no meaningful new angle.

---

# 22. Fake Personalization Check

Reject unsupported statements such as:

> I noticed you're expanding.

> I saw you're hiring.

> I came across your recent projects.

unless the information is explicitly available.

---

# 23. Template Check

Compare against reference examples.

The email should demonstrate similar quality and reasoning but should not mechanically copy:

- Sentence structure
- Wording
- Opening
- CTA
- Business example

---

# 24. Repetition Check

Check for unnecessary repetition of:

- Exact phrases
- Sentence openings
- Audience descriptions
- Value language
- CTA wording

Natural reuse is acceptable.

Mechanical reuse is not.

---

# 25. Deliverability Check

Verify that Email 3 does not contain obvious unnecessary deliverability risks.

Avoid:

- Excessive punctuation
- ALL CAPS
- Hype language
- Excessive promotional language
- Multiple links
- Calendar links
- Images
- Tracking-heavy formatting
- Unnecessary HTML-style formatting

Keep it plain and conversational.

---

# 26. Product Isolation Check

Verify that the email uses only PersistIQ's value proposition.

Do not reference:

- BeSeries
- SalesCloser
- Invigo
- Other products
- Other product-specific benefits

---

# 27. ICP Check

Do not perform ICP qualification in QA.

The lead is assumed to be qualified upstream.

QA should evaluate whether the message is relevant to the business, not whether the business belongs in the ICP.

---

# 28. Final Recipient Test

Read the complete sequence:

Email 1
→ Email 2
→ Email 3

Then ask:

> Would the recipient understand why Email 3 is different from the previous two?

If yes, continue.

If no, rewrite Email 3.

---

# 29. Final Quality Gate

Email 3 passes only if all are true:

- [ ] New commercial angle
- [ ] Relevant to same business
- [ ] Connected to actual service
- [ ] Supported by evidence or reasonable inference
- [ ] No fabricated facts
- [ ] No fake personalization
- [ ] PersistIQ value is natural
- [ ] One primary commercial idea
- [ ] Concise
- [ ] Human and conversational
- [ ] No hype
- [ ] No guarantees
- [ ] No aggressive CTA
- [ ] No generic follow-up
- [ ] No unnecessary links
- [ ] No product mixing
- [ ] No ICP qualification
- [ ] No mechanical template copying
- [ ] No unnecessary repetition
- [ ] Feels like a natural third email

Only output Email 3 after all required checks pass.

---

# 30. Final QA Principle

Email 3 should pass the simplest test:

**New reason. Same business. Same service. Natural PersistIQ value. Low pressure.**
