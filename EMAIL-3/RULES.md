# Email 3 — Rules

## 1. Core Rule

Email 3 must introduce a **new, legitimate commercial angle or additional reason to engage**.

It must not simply rewrite, shorten, or repeat Email 1 or Email 2.

---

## 2. Sequence Context

Email 3 must read and consider:

- Email 1
- Email 2
- The business
- The company's service
- The PersistIQ value proposition

The final Email 3 must fit naturally after the first two messages.

---

## 3. Same Business

Email 3 must remain about the same business.

Do not change the business context simply to create a new angle.

---

## 4. Same Core Service

The new angle must remain connected to the service the company actually provides.

The angle may change.

The company's service does not.

---

## 5. New Angle Requirement

Email 3 must add something meaningfully different from the previous messages.

Valid differences can include:

- A different customer situation
- A different audience
- A different commercial trigger
- A different service-related need
- A different prospecting opportunity
- A different reason customers may seek the service

---

## 6. No Forced Novelty

Do not invent a new angle just because Email 3 requires one.

The new angle must make commercial sense.

If an angle cannot be supported by the available information or reasonable inference, do not use it.

---

## 7. No Repetition

Do not repeat:

- The same opening idea
- The same customer situation
- The same commercial trigger
- The same explanation of value
- The same sentence structure

Minor natural overlap is acceptable.

The overall commercial idea should be different.

---

## 8. Email 2 Is Not a New Angle

Email 2 is a follow-up to Email 1.

Email 3 is different.

Do not make Email 3 another follow-up such as:

- Just following up again
- Wanted to check back
- Circling back
- Any thoughts on this?

A follow-up alone does not satisfy the Email 3 requirement.

---

## 9. Commercial Connection

The new angle must answer:

"Why could this situation create a legitimate opportunity for this business?"

If that connection is unclear, reject the angle.

---

## 10. Evidence

Use the same evidence hierarchy established for the rest of the system.

Allowed:

- Explicit information
- Strong category-level inference
- Reasonable contextual inference

Not allowed:

- Invented company facts
- Invented customers
- Invented projects
- Invented demand
- Invented growth
- Invented problems
- Invented intent

---

## 11. Cautious Inference

When the new angle is inferred, use cautious language.

Preferred:

- may
- might
- could
- often
- businesses considering
- companies evaluating
- people looking for
- customers planning

Do not state inferred situations as confirmed facts.

---

## 12. PersistIQ Connection

Email 3 should still connect the opportunity to PersistIQ.

Natural value concepts include:

- Create more conversations
- Reach relevant prospects
- Surface relevant opportunities
- Connect with potential customers
- Reach businesses considering the service

Do not force the exact same wording into every email.

---

## 13. One Primary Idea

Email 3 should contain one primary commercial idea.

Do not combine several unrelated angles into one message.

Bad:

"Companies may need your service, you could reach new customers, improve retention, hire staff, and expand into new markets."

Better:

One clear additional commercial opportunity.

---

## 14. Concision

Keep Email 3 concise.

Prefer:

- One or two sentences
- One primary commercial angle
- One PersistIQ value connection
- One soft CTA

Do not over-explain the reasoning.

---

## 15. Soft CTA

End with a low-pressure CTA.

Examples:

- Worth exploring?
- Worth a look?
- Open to exploring it?
- Make sense to discuss?
- Open to seeing how?

Do not use aggressive meeting requests.

---

## 16. No Aggressive Escalation

Do not use:

- Last chance
- Final attempt
- Final follow-up
- Act now
- Don't miss out
- Urgent
- When are you free?
- Book a call
- Schedule a meeting

Email 3 remains conversational and low pressure.

---

## 17. No Generic Follow-Up

Avoid messages that contain little or no commercial value.

Examples:

- Any thoughts?
- Just checking in.
- Following up again.
- Wanted to bump this.
- Did you get a chance to see this?
- Should I close the loop?

These do not qualify as Email 3.

---

## 18. No Generic Pitch

Do not remove the business context and fall back to:

"We help companies generate leads."

"We can help you get more customers."

"We provide outbound email solutions."

The new angle must explain **why this business could have a relevant opportunity**.

---

## 19. No Fake Personalization

Do not pretend to know something that has not been established.

Avoid:

"I noticed you're expanding."

"I saw you're looking for new clients."

"I noticed you're struggling with..."

"I saw you're launching..."

unless the information is explicitly supported.

---

## 20. No "I Noticed" Opener

Do not begin Email 3 with:

- I noticed
- I came across
- I saw that
- I was impressed by

Use direct commercial relevance instead.

---

## 21. No Hype

Avoid:

- Game-changing
- Revolutionary
- Guaranteed
- Explosive growth
- Skyrocket
- Dominate
- Massive
- Incredible
- Cutting-edge
- Best-in-class
- Unlock
- 10x
- Supercharge

Use simple, credible language.

---

## 22. No Guarantees

Never guarantee:

- Leads
- Meetings
- Revenue
- Customers
- Growth
- Response rates
- Results

Use "could help", "may create", "could surface", or similar cautious language.

---

## 23. No Template Copying

Do not copy an Email 1 or reference example and simply substitute:

- Business type
- Service
- Audience
- Customer

Examples are reference material, not templates.

---

## 24. Natural Variation

Variation should come from the actual business and new angle.

Do not randomize:

- Synonyms
- Sentence openings
- CTA wording
- Adjectives

for the purpose of appearing different.

Meaning comes first.

---

## 25. No Unnecessary Links

Do not include:

- Calendar links
- Tracking links
- Unnecessary URLs
- Images
- Signatures

unless explicitly required by the production system.

---

## 26. No Product Mixing

Email 3 must use the PersistIQ value proposition.

Do not introduce value propositions belonging to:

- BeSeries
- SalesCloser
- Invigo
- Another product
- Another campaign

---

## 27. No ICP Logic

ICP qualification is performed upstream.

Email 3 must not decide whether the lead is:

- ICP
- Non-ICP
- Qualified
- Unqualified

Assume the incoming lead has already passed qualification.

---

## 28. Website and Business Evidence

When website information is available, use it to understand:

- What the business does
- Who it serves
- What services it provides
- What customer situations may create demand
- What additional commercial angles are plausible

Do not turn website observations into fake personalization.

---

## 29. Angle Selection Priority

When selecting a new angle, prioritize:

1. Explicit business information
2. Strong service/customer relationship
3. Strong commercial inference
4. Reasonable contextual inference

Do not choose a weaker angle simply because it sounds more creative.

---

## 30. Final Test

Before approving Email 3, ask:

### Test 1
Is this genuinely different from Email 1?

### Test 2
Is this genuinely different from Email 2?

### Test 3
Is it still relevant to the same business?

### Test 4
Is it connected to the company's actual service?

### Test 5
Is the commercial opportunity believable?

### Test 6
Is the PersistIQ connection natural?

### Test 7
Is the language cautious where inference is involved?

### Test 8
Is it concise?

### Test 9
Does it avoid hype and pressure?

### Test 10
Would this sound natural as the third email in a real outbound sequence?

If any answer is no, revise the email before output.

---

## 31. Final Principle

Email 3 should feel like:

**"There is another legitimate reason this could be relevant to you."**

It should not feel like:

**"I'm repeating the same pitch because I need a third email."**
