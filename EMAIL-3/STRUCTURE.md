# Email 3 — Structure

## 1. Core Structure

Email 3 follows this logic:

**New commercial angle → PersistIQ value → Soft CTA**

The message should introduce a different legitimate reason for the prospect to engage while remaining connected to the same business and service.

---

## 2. Sentence 1 — New Angle

The first sentence introduces the additional commercial opportunity.

It should establish:

- A relevant audience, situation, or trigger
- A plausible need
- A connection to the company's service

The sentence should make the new angle understandable without explaining the entire reasoning process.

---

## 3. Sentence 2 — PersistIQ Value

The second sentence connects the new angle to PersistIQ.

It should communicate that PersistIQ could help:

- Create conversations
- Reach relevant prospects
- Surface opportunities
- Connect with potential customers
- Reach businesses considering the service

Use the value expression that fits the angle naturally.

---

## 4. CTA

End with a soft CTA.

The CTA should invite consideration rather than pressure the prospect into a meeting.

Examples:

- Worth exploring?
- Worth a look?
- Open to exploring it?
- Make sense to discuss?
- Open to seeing how?

---

## 5. Recommended Flow

The preferred flow is:

**Additional customer situation**
→ **relevant business service**
→ **PersistIQ could help create relevant conversations**
→ **soft CTA**

---

## 6. Example

If Email 1 focused on:

**Property owners dealing with aging roofs**

Email 3 could introduce:

**Property owners planning broader exterior improvements**

Then connect that opportunity to the roofing company's service and PersistIQ.

The angle is different, but the business relationship remains logical.

---

## 7. Do Not Use a Fixed Template

The structure is a reasoning framework, not a sentence template.

Do not repeatedly generate:

> [Audience] may need [service]. We could help [business] reach more of them. Worth exploring?

Instead, adapt the language to:

- The business
- The service
- The new angle
- The audience
- The commercial situation

---

## 8. Email 3 Should Feel New

The reader should be able to distinguish Email 3 from Email 1 immediately.

Email 1:

**Initial commercial reason**

Email 2:

**Follow-up on that reason**

Email 3:

**Additional commercial reason**

---

## 9. Avoid Rebuilding Context

Do not restate:

- The company description
- The full service offering
- The Email 1 argument
- The Email 2 follow-up

The prospect already received that information.

Introduce the new angle directly.

---

## 10. One Commercial Idea

Email 3 should focus on one additional opportunity.

Do not stack multiple angles into one email.

Bad:

> Businesses may need your service, new customers may be looking for it, and you could also expand into new markets.

Better:

Focus on one specific, commercially relevant opportunity.

---

## 11. Length

Default to **one or two concise sentences**.

Do not make Email 3 longer than necessary.

Shorter is acceptable when the new angle can be expressed clearly without losing the PersistIQ connection.

---

## 12. Tone

Maintain the established PersistIQ tone:

- Human
- Professional
- Conversational
- Direct
- Understated
- Commercially aware
- Low pressure

Do not make Email 3 more aggressive simply because it is later in the sequence.

---

## 13. Evidence-to-Structure Mapping

Use the strongest available information.

### Explicit information

Use directly when commercially relevant.

### Strong category inference

Use naturally with appropriate language.

### Reasonable contextual inference

Use cautiously.

### Unsupported assumption

Do not use.

---

## 14. Angle Selection Before Writing

Before drafting, determine:

1. What angle was used in Email 1?
2. What was Email 2 following up on?
3. What additional angle is available?
4. Why does that angle matter commercially?
5. How does the company's service address it?
6. How can PersistIQ help create relevant conversations around it?

Only then write the email.

---

## 15. Final Structure Test

A completed Email 3 should satisfy:

**New angle**
+
**Business/service relevance**
+
**PersistIQ value**
+
**Soft CTA**

If any component is missing, revise before output.

---

## 16. Sequence Example

### Email 1

> Property owners facing aging or storm-damaged roofs may need the inspections and replacements you provide. We could help surface more nearby projects worth discussing.

### Email 2

> Just following up on this. We could help bring more relevant roofing opportunities to your team.

### Email 3

> Commercial properties planning broader exterior work may also need roofing support as projects develop. We could help connect you with more of those conversations. Worth exploring?

The third email introduces a **different commercial situation** rather than repeating the original storm/aging-roof angle.

---

## 17. Final Principle

Email 3 should move the conversation forward by adding **new relevance**, not by adding pressure.

The prospect should finish the email thinking:

**"That is another reason this could be relevant."**
