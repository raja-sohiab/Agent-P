# PersistIQ Email Sequence Logic

## 1. Purpose

This document defines how Agent-P should treat the three-email outbound sequence.

The emails are connected.

They should not be generated as three independent messages.

The sequence is:

Email 1
→ Initial relevance + value

Email 2
→ Follow-up based on Email 1

Email 3
→ New angle / additional reason to engage

---

# 2. Sequence Principle

The sequence should progressively develop the commercial proposition.

Email 1 establishes the initial reason for relevance.

Email 2 follows up on that proposition.

Email 3 introduces another legitimate reason the prospect may want to engage.

The sequence should progress without becoming repetitive or aggressive.

---

# 3. Email 1 — Initial Relevance + Value

Email 1 is the foundation of the sequence.

It establishes:

- Business relevance
- Audience
- Customer situation or need
- Relevant service
- PersistIQ value
- Soft CTA

Core reasoning:

Business
→ Audience
→ Situation / Need
→ Service
→ PersistIQ Value
→ Soft CTA

Email 1 should normally be concise and two sentences.

---

# 4. Email 2 — Follow-up Based on Email 1

Email 2 follows directly from Email 1.

It should remind the prospect of the original proposition without repeating the complete Email 1.

Email 2 should:

- Be short
- Be concise
- Be direct
- Preserve the original business context
- Reinforce the original value
- Use a soft CTA

Email 2 should not:

- Restart the pitch
- Introduce an unrelated angle
- Copy Email 1
- Become more aggressive
- Add unsupported claims
- Become unnecessarily long

Conceptual flow:

Email 1 proposition
→ Short follow-up
→ Reinforced value
→ Soft CTA

---

# 5. Email 3 — New Angle / Additional Reason

Email 3 introduces a genuinely new commercially relevant angle.

It should provide another legitimate reason for the prospect to consider engaging. Prefer a different customer situation, commercial trigger, service-related need, decision point, service application, or commercial opportunity when it provides the stronger reason.

The new angle must still relate to:

- The same business
- Its actual service
- Its audience
- A plausible commercial situation
- PersistIQ's value

Conceptual flow:

Original business context
→ New relevant commercial angle
→ PersistIQ value
→ Soft CTA

Email 3 is not simply a recap of Email 1 and Email 2.

---

# 6. Sequence Memory

Sequence context is mandatory.

When generating Email 2, Agent-P should have access to:

- The lead information
- The business understanding
- The generated Email 1

When generating Email 3, Agent-P should have access to:

- The lead information
- The business understanding
- Email 1
- Email 2

The agent must consider what has already been communicated.

---

# 7. What Must Remain Consistent

Across all three emails, maintain consistency in:

- Business understanding
- Relevant service
- Core PersistIQ value
- Commercial context
- Professional tone
- Soft CTA style

The underlying proposition remains consistent.

The messaging angle may change.

---

# 8. What Should Change

The sequence should not repeat the same message three times.

### Email 1

Establish the initial reason.

### Email 2

Follow up on that reason.

### Email 3

Provide another relevant reason.

The prospect should encounter progression rather than repetition.

---

# 9. Email 2 Angle Selection

Email 2 should generally remain close to the original Email 1 proposition.

Possible changes include:

- Shorter framing
- More direct wording
- Slightly different description of the same audience
- Reinforcement of the same customer situation
- Reinforcement of the same opportunity

Do not introduce a new unrelated commercial problem.

---

# 10. Email 3 Angle Selection

Email 3 has more flexibility.

A new angle may come from:

- Another customer situation
- Another commercial trigger
- Another service-related need
- Another reason prospects may require the company's service
- Another way the business could benefit from relevant conversations

Do not create another audience merely to satisfy the new-angle requirement. An audience-based angle may be used only when it is genuinely the strongest new commercial angle and independently supported by the available business/service evidence. Do not use the formulaic "another relevant audience" construction. Prefer other valid angles when they provide a stronger commercial reason to engage.

The new angle must be commercially connected to the business.

---

# 11. Valid Angle Progression

Example:

### Email 1

Property owners with aging roofs may be considering repairs or replacement.

PersistIQ could help create conversations with people considering that work.

### Email 2

Follow up on reaching property owners who may be evaluating roofing work.

### Email 3

Introduce another angle, such as reaching property owners planning broader exterior improvements where roofing services may become relevant.

The angle changes.

The business and PersistIQ value remain consistent.

---

# 12. Invalid Angle Progression

Example:

### Email 1

Roofing prospects.

### Email 2

Roofing prospects.

### Email 3

Hiring employees.

This is invalid because the new angle is unrelated to the commercial proposition.

---

# 13. No Artificial Escalation

Later emails should not become more aggressive.

Do not use the sequence:

Email 1:
Soft

Email 2:
More sales pressure

Email 3:
Urgency / last chance

Instead:

Email 1:
Initial relevance

Email 2:
Follow-up

Email 3:
Additional relevant angle

All three remain low pressure.

---

# 14. CTA Consistency

Each email should use a soft CTA.

The CTA wording may vary.

Do not automatically escalate from:

"Worth exploring?"

to:

"Can we book a call?"

The sequence should remain conversational.

---

# 15. Repetition Control

Agent-P must track what has already been said.

Avoid repeating:

- The same opening
- The same sentence
- The same value phrase
- The same customer situation
- The same CTA
- The same wording

However, the underlying commercial proposition may remain consistent.

---

# 16. New Angle Does Not Mean New Product

Email 3 may introduce a new reason to engage.

It must not introduce:

- A different product
- A different product value proposition
- An unrelated service
- An unrelated campaign objective

Agent-P represents PersistIQ throughout the sequence.

---

# 17. New Angle Does Not Mean Fabrication

The agent must not invent a new angle simply because Email 3 requires one.

The new angle must come from:

- Actual business information
- Strong category-level knowledge
- Reasonable commercial inference

Unsupported company-specific claims remain prohibited.

---

# 18. Website Reuse

Website information may be used across multiple emails when relevant.

However, do not repeatedly restate the same website information.

Use the website to understand the business and identify legitimate commercial angles.

---

# 19. Sequence Independence

Each email should make sense on its own.

However, the emails should still feel connected when read together.

The prospect should be able to understand Email 2 or Email 3 without needing to reconstruct the entire previous message.

---

# 20. Sequence Priority

When generating an email, prioritize:

1. Correct business understanding
2. Sequence position
3. Relevant commercial angle
4. PersistIQ value
5. Natural language
6. Concision
7. Soft CTA
8. Variation

---

# 21. Email 4

Email 4 is not currently defined.

Do not create Email 4 rules, patterns, examples, or messaging logic until a separate specification is provided.

---

# 22. Final Sequence Model

The complete current sequence is:

EMAIL 1
Initial relevance + value

↓

EMAIL 2
Follow-up based on Email 1

↓

EMAIL 3
New angle / additional reason to engage

The sequence should feel like one coherent conversation rather than three unrelated pitches.
